# Make the look follow the story
When: the theme should change with the state (night, danger, corruption, affection, a location), or a character's portrait should change with their mood, outfit or health.

1. **Read first.** Read the core API's "Moods and portraits" section (read_doc core-api#moods-and-portraits-cf-mood-cf-ui-portrait), then read_theme and read_values. A mood or a portrait follows a value: add the value with set_value first if the card lacks it (a choice is best, so the options are known).
2. **Pick few moods that matter.** Two to four: the ones the player should feel (danger rising, night falling, a curse spreading). Each follows one value: `is` for named states, `min`/`max` for a number crossing a line. Order them from calm to intense, since a later mood wins.
3. **Change little, on purpose.** Two or three tokens (bg and surface darker for night, the accent to `--cf-bad`'s red for danger), a 10 to 20% tint, a texture only when it tells something (Vignette for dread). Keep contrast: text must stay readable on the new bg. At most one mood has an ambience, and only weather the story has.
4. **Bind the portraits.** set_portrait for each character the screens show often: their emotion value, outfit value and a hurt line on their health. In screens, draw them with `app.portrait(name)`; don't build portrait URLs by hand for them.
5. **Get the art.** read_theme lists the emotions and outfits with no image (portrait_art_missing). Ask for the important ones with request_asset (one per emotion: neutral, the two or three the story uses most, and hurt) and tell the user they're on the shot list. Until then the portrait falls back to the closest image or the initial, so the screen still works.
6. **Look at it.** preview_states with a state for each mood (and one with none), on the phone, light and dark: the colours must still pass contrast, and the change must be visible but calm. Run lint: it says when a mood or a portrait follows a value that doesn't exist.
